What drives deep runs

Bar length is how far each stat moved the model’s expected stage on average, in knockout rounds (mean absolute SHAP value, measured only on seasons the model didn’t train on). The top 6 carry a label. Robust: a simpler logistic model agrees on the direction in at least 12 of 15 seasons, and the stat points the same way on its own. Conditional: the direction holds only with the other stats held fixed; on its own the stat points the other way or barely at all. Model-dependent: the simpler model disagrees too often.

AI summary

✓All figures found in the data

The model trained on 256 knockout team-seasons using group and league-phase stats achieved a mean within-season Spearman correlation of 0.45 with a 95% interval from 0.34 to 0.54, an AUC for reaching the final of 0.73 with an interval from 0.61 to 0.83, and a Brier score of 0.098 compared to a base-rate guess of 0.103. The resulting Brier skill was 0.05 with an interval from -0.04 to 0.13; because this interval includes zero, the edge over the base-rate guess is inconclusive on that measure. Meanwhile, 46.7% of actual finalists appeared in the model's top four, compared to a random ranking share of 23.9%; since the 95% interval for the model's share ranges from 30.0 to 60.0 and does not include the random value, the whole interval lies above it.

Deep runs are associated with stats where pedigree, results, or style matter most, though feature-set comparisons show overlapping intervals so differences between these categories are tentative. The top stat is Attacks per game from the style group with an importance of 0.229 knockout stages and a robustness label of robust. Goal difference per game follows as a results stat with an importance of 0.198 and a robust label, while Long passes (% of passes) is a conditional style stat with an importance of 0.135 that points the other way on its own. Passes per game is another robust style stat at 0.119, whereas Fouls committed per game is model-dependent at 0.094 and Shot conversion is conditional at 0.086.

Method and caveats

  • Data: UEFA’s public match, team-statistics and club-coefficient feeds for 15 seasons (2011-12 to 2025-26). That covers 488 group or league-phase team-seasons, of which 256 reached the knockouts.
  • Inputs: 15 per-game stats from group and league-phase matches only, standardised within each season, plus the club’s five-year UEFA coefficient from before the season. Save rate was dropped because the 2011-12 feed lacks saves for too many matches.
  • Validation: each season is predicted by models trained on the other 14. The ranking correlation averages 0.45 (95% interval 0.34 to 0.54), and the AUC for reaching the final is 0.73 (0.61 to 0.83). 47% of finalists were in their season’s predicted top four (30% to 60%), against 24% for a random ranking. Against always guessing the base rate, the probabilities have a Brier skill of 0.05; its interval (-0.04 to 0.13) includes zero, so that edge is inconclusive. Intervals come from resampling seasons.
  • Probabilities of reaching the final are scaled within each season to add up to two, because exactly two teams get there.
  • AI write-ups: qwen/qwen3.5-35b-a3b running locally in LM Studio, given only the numbers behind this page. Every figure it writes is checked against those numbers. The check confirms that the figure appears in the data, not that it is attached to the right stat, and the wording itself is not checked.

Caveats

  • Correlation is not causation, and group or league-phase stats depend on the opponents drawn.
  • Knockout football is high-variance. The 2026 final was decided on penalties.
  • Real Madrid won 6 of the 15 finals, so one club’s profile weighs heavily on what winning looks like.
  • In 2024-25 and 2025-26 a top-eight league finish skips the play-off, which builds in an advantage for results.
  • UEFA publishes no expected-goals data for these seasons, and the shot counts leave out blocked shots.
  • UEFA’s feed reported possession in seconds and distance in metres for some 2014-16 matches. These were converted using the feed’s own figures, and partial tracking and placeholder zeros were ignored.
  • Some stat definitions changed between seasons, so comparisons with all teams since 2011-12 mix them. The model itself only compares teams within a season.
  • 15 seasons contain only 30 finalists, so the probabilities are rough.
  • Models are trained on seasons after the one being scored as well as before it.
  • UEFA’s APIs are undocumented. Their values are used as published.
  • The AI text is limited to the numbers and checked against them, but its interpretations are not causal evidence.
